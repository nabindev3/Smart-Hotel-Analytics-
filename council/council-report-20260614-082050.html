Question: Analyze the whole project, rate it out of 10, and check for bugs. Context: a portfolio/reference repo for a job search, trained entirely on synthetic data.

Where the council agrees

  • The synthetic data is a closed loop. Every headline metric (0.81 AUC, 15% MAPE) measures fit to a world the author generated — to a sharp interviewer they're unfalsifiable. 3 of 5 advisors hit this independently.
  • The real value isn't the ML — it's the judgment. Removing leakage and defending the lower AUC, killing the over-engineered LP, the ADR log + bug retrospective. That's senior-signal and rare in a portfolio.
  • Rate it as a hiring artifact, not a product. On that axis it's strong; as a revenue system it's a toy.

Where the council clashes

Substance camp (Contrarian, Outsider)

Rebuild at least the cancellation model on the real public Antonio hotel-bookings dataset. That converts "fit-to-simulation" into a defensible result and is the only thing that survives a technical interview.

Presentation camp (Executor, Expansionist)

Don't touch the ML. The repo is judged in 30 seconds — fix the cold-start/demo and reposition the README to lead with the decisions log. Real-data swap is high-effort and not what's blocking interviews today.

Blind spot the council caught

  • "Removing AI tells" is self-defeating. Two advisors flagged that actively concealing how the project was built undermines its one differentiator — demonstrated honesty. If an interviewer senses sanitized provenance, every honest disclosure (honest AUC, the retrospective) reads as selective. Defend your judgment; don't hide the workflow.

Recommendation

Do the presentation fixes now (this week) and the one substantive upgrade next. Short-term ROI is in the demo: a recruiter never reaches your 48 tests if the link hangs for 50 seconds. But the Contrarian is right that the real-data swap is the difference between a 7 and an 8+ — it's the one change that survives interview scrutiny. Stop polishing prose to hide AI assistance; that effort is negative-value.

The one thing to do first

Kill the cold-start first impression: record a 30-second Loom of you driving the dashboard and narrating one ML decision (e.g. "why I removed leakage"), and put it at the very top of the README — plus a keep-warm cron ping. That decouples "did they see it work" from "is the free-tier server awake."

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian 6/10

Every metric measures how well a model fits a world the author invented — a closed loop. 0.81 AUC means "XGBoost can reverse-engineer my synthetic regressors." The recommender (SVD over a rule-based matrix) makes the circularity nakedly visible. "Honest labeling" is good ethics but bad portfolio strategy. Fix: use the real public Antonio hotel-bookings dataset for at least the cancellation model → an 8. Secondary risk: "removing AI tells" makes honest disclosures read as selective. Biggest risk: the results section is unfalsifiable; a sharp interviewer notices in 90 seconds.

The First Principles Thinker 7/10

Wrong thing to rate. As an ML product it's a 2 (closed loop). But it's a hiring artifact; the deliverable is evidence of judgment — removing leakage for an honest 0.81, deleting the LP, the ADR log, the retrospective. Synthetic data is honest scoping, not the weakness. The weakness is the instinct to hide how it was made: the value prop is demonstrated honesty, so concealing AI-assisted provenance collapses the differentiator. Document the AI-assisted workflow as a deliberate choice and the same project gets stronger.

The Expansionist 8/10 (9 repositioned)

The most undervalued asset is the retrospective + ADR log, not the ML. Mid-level engineers are a commodity; what's hard to show is judgment under ambiguity — and this shows it (caught leakage in your own headline metric and defended the lower number; killed your own darling LP). Reposition: the README's first 200 words should lead with "I keep a decisions log and a bug retrospective — here are 3 real mistakes I caught." Every bug is a pre-rehearsed STAR story and a potential blog post / lightning talk.

The Outsider 7 → 5/10

90 seconds cold: slick demo, real API docs, clean repo, lots of writing → "this person ships." Then the disclaimer. Stating it is ethical (hiding would be worse), but the implication hurts: synthetic-script data means the model learns rules the script wrote — the headline numbers are tautologies. And the scope (six products) on fake data reads like avoiding the hard part (wrangling real data). Fix: drop synthetic numbers from the headline; lead with the engineering and decisions log; rebuild one feature on a real public dataset and make it the star.

The Executor 7/10

A sleeping demo judged in 30 seconds. Monday: kill the cold-start. A recruiter waits 50s on a blank free-tier screen and closes the tab before the 48 tests matter. Pick one: a GitHub Actions cron pinging the URL every 10 min, or a 30-second Loom of you driving the dashboard at the top of the README (decouples "did they see it work" from "is the server awake"). Do NOT touch the ML / swap data / add models — "polishing a car with a dead battery." Cheap extra: a dashboard GIF above the fold.
